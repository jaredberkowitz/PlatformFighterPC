# Art review 8: what still reads flat (2026-10-18)

After art review 7 (built sea wall, canyon ledges, shaped earth fronts, rolled lips, palms, sea swell, trunk bark, sand ripples, mountain
gullies, brass rail, island keels, umbrella). Match-camera shots: `docs/screenshots/review8_all_stages.jpg` (meadow, canyon, beach,
grove, city). Same direction: depth and form on what is there, no new detail.

## Ranked by screen area × flatness

1. **The floating platforms (every stage, the middle of the view).** Still thin slabs with flat faces: the meadow's yellow tops with plain
   red sides, the canyon's and grove's boards, the city's pink girders. They stay floating (owner); refine their form: bevelled, slightly
   uneven edges, plank seams with depth, the side faces falling into shade, a soft lit lip along the top front edge.
2. **The lawn tops (meadow, grove).** The largest single surface after the fronts: a saturated lime with faint patches; at match
   distance the blades and patches barely read. Stronger, larger tonal shapes (a cool shade toward the back edge, warm light in front)
   rather than more texture.
3. **The grove's lower tier.** The stepped block under the grove stage is still a flat painted face (the top tier is shaped now); shape
   it the same way, and give each step a lit top edge.
4. **The far land (meadow hills, canyon dunes and far range).** Flat single-colour shapes under heavy haze; a lit crest and a shaded
   side on each, as the mountains now have.
5. **City skyline.** The background towers are flat boxes with painted windows; their silhouettes step, but each face is one value.
   A lit edge on the corner facing the moon and darker side faces would turn them into blocks without adding detail.
6. **Small props.** Hay bales, barrels, crates and the stump read well; the meadow's sign and the canyon's crate are the flattest.

## Suggested order

1, then 2 and 3 together (grassy stages), then 4 and 5, then 6. Screenshot before and after at the match camera; owner review.
