## In the match capture
- At the match camera the fighters read as fighting. Side-on bodies give clearer silhouettes and leave the facing obvious. The faces
  still read.
- Fighters are small at the default camera (about a sixth of the screen's height). Fine detail in the poses barely shows; what reads
  there is silhouette, weight and timing. That argues for the remaining work being about timing, not more pose detail.

## Weakest visuals now, ranked
1. **Timing against the reference: holds, overshoot and settle.** This is the remaining part of the owner's "weightless" note. Poses are
   now right; how long a strike holds, how far the follow-through carries and how the body settles have not been measured frame by
   frame against the reference gifs. Next animation round: measure the Longsword's and Claws' main moves against them and fix the shared
   timing curves first.
2. **Run foot slide** (0.4–0.75 of ground speed). The brawler family shares the main run, and the sword family has no run of its own.
3. **The hurt reaction** holds one thrown-back pose too long. It needs a recoil, then a recovery, scaled by how hard the hit was.
4. **The faces still covered:** the Greatsword's two-handed grip (81 frames) and the Settler's balloon float (52 frames). The float is a
   design call: re-pose the hold or change the body proportions.
5. **The canyon's buttes and floating top platform** (stage session).
6. **Roster identity:** a signature silhouette piece per built-in fighter.
