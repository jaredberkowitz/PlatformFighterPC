World-building: making each stage a place

The stages look like paper; now they should be believable places, each with a reason to exist and signs of life. The story sheet (docs/art/stages/WORLD.md) gives every stage its place and what it lacks; the beach and canyon are mocked first. The match camera sees the beach's water and the canyon's floor only far out, so everything there is built large. Choose a stage, a view and an option; press 1 to 4 to switch in place. Options can be combined.

A fishing harbour

Missing: Nobody uses the pier: no boats at it, no catch, no birds, no one in the town.

OptionWhat it adds
A · A working pierA fishing boat moored to a buoy off the hut, nets over its side and its catch in crates; nets and crates on the boardwalk
B · Harbour lifeGulls perched on the lamp, the crates and the life ring's post, more gliding over the water; a rowboat by the sailboat
C · The town at workMarket awnings, townsfolk and bunting on the quay, chimney smoke: the most story, but far off

My pick: A and B together: the pier's purpose and its life. With motion (gulls wheeling, the boats bobbing) they would be the most alive.

A desert crossing at dusk

Missing: The crossing itself: the floor between the stage and the buttes is empty sand, and nothing says anyone passes.

OptionWhat it adds
A · A railroadA timber trestle across the floor behind the buttes, a little train under its smoke, a water tower by the track
B · A wagon trailA pale trail winding to the horizon, telegraph poles along it, a covered wagon
C · A ranchA windpump and its tank, a corral with cattle, an adobe house with chimney smoke

My pick: A, the railroad: it fills the empty floor most and gives the frame a strong line behind the buttes.
