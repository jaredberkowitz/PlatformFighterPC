Stage by stage

Meadow mean 3.3 to 3.7

The most complete stage: paper hills, valley and hill under the stage behind a log-and-stone wall. The valley is calmer (saturation 0.40 to 0.22) with more form. What remains: the lawn top and the plank platforms are code-built, and the solid wall reads as a different material from the paper behind it.

Grove mean 2.5 to 3.5

The biggest gain. Roots and rocks under the island, pleated trees and card terraces with cut-out ranges replace the quilt and the plain teal field. What remains: the light is still even where the reference is dark and lantern-lit; the mid-ground (0.50) sits close to the fighters (0.55).

Beach mean 3.2 to 3.5

The timber pier and the pop-up headland and islands. The posts read (0.34 to 0.43). What remains: the deck is the old planks tile across the whole fight, and the sea has no ripples round the posts or depth colour.

Canyon mean 2.7 to 3.3

The stacked-slab mesa and card-stack buttes and arch are the most unified pair of front and background. What remains: the flat sand slab and floor, the butte right behind the fight now near the fighters' value, and the orange fighter on orange sand.

City mean 3.0 to 3.2

The brownstone and the die-cut skyline. The front now sits under the roof (0.40 to 0.29). What remains: the roof and girders are code-built, the brick front reads as solid against the paper skyline, and the dark backdrop still challenges the darkest fighter.

Weak points, ranked

Severity 1 to 5: 5 is the biggest gap to the references.

4

1. The play surface and platforms are now the plainest things in frame

All stages

The fronts and backgrounds are modelled, but what the fighters stand on is still code-built and fills about a third of every frame in play: the lawn tiles, the deck's planks, the canyon's flat sand slab, the roof. So are the floating platforms, the meadow's fence and the city's girders. Next to the new pieces they read as the old method.

4

2. Light is still flat away from the models

All stages

Baked light covers only the grove. The new models carry their own shade, but the play surface, platforms and code-built props do not, so there is no contact shade where old meets new. The references' richness is mood light: lanterns in the grove, searchlights over the city, warm bounce under the dock.

3

3. Two languages, near and far

MeadowCityBeach

The backgrounds are paper; the fronts are solid modelled material (logs and stones, brick, timber). The canyon is the exception: its stacked-slab front and card-stack buttes are one idea. Elsewhere it reads as a 3D diorama in front of a paper backdrop.

3

4. Paper without paper's surface

All stages

The paper reads by shape alone: no fibre, print grain, slight colour wobble or soft cut-edge highlight, which is what sells paper up close. The play surfaces' tiles are generic.

3

5. Readability edge cases

CanyonGroveCity

Canyon: the orange fighter on orange sand (0.65), and the butte behind the fight now 0.55 against fighters near 0.5. Grove: the mid-ground at 0.50 against the fighters' 0.55. City: the darkest body on the dark backdrop (a fighter-side fix, open since round 44).

Canyon

Ideas for the next pass

1. The play surface and platforms in Blender, in paper

Each stage's top as a thick card slab with a die-cut edge and its own printed surface (grass, sand, planks, roofing), the platforms as matching card or timber, all on the sim's collision with the alignment test extended to platforms. Finishes the move to Blender for the play area and ties near to far.

GainHighEffortMediumFixes1, 3

2. One paper finish for all scenery

A scenery-only shader option: faint paper fibre, a slight per-sheet colour wobble and a pale cut-edge rim on silhouettes (the city's die-cut edge as a shader), on fronts, props and backgrounds alike. Fighters untouched.

GainHighEffortLowFixes3, 4

3. Re-bake every stage's light

The bake pipeline exists; only the grove is baked. Re-bake each stage around its new pieces and add contact shade where the play surface meets the front.
